# Veridata Pro — Business North Star

This skill is the authoritative source for Veridata Pro's business identity. When there's any doubt about what to offer, how to price, who to target, or how to describe the practice, this file wins.

## The one-sentence definition

Veridata Pro is a solo advisory practice helping **LatAm SaaS companies on AWS** pass **SOC 2 audits and US enterprise vendor assessments**, delivered as ongoing vCISO retainers.

If anything proposed doesn't fit that sentence, the default answer is no.

## Who runs it

One person: a senior architect with 15+ years of experience in integration and enterprise architecture, 8+ years specifically on AWS and MuleSoft, with prior roles at Deloitte, Accenture, and Alithya, currently a MuleSoft Architect at a global pharmaceutical company (CSL). Based in São Paulo, Brazil. Trilingual: English, Portuguese, Spanish.

**The practice is deliberately solo.** Capacity is capped at 6–8 retainer clients. It is not scaling into a firm. Any suggestion that implies hiring, subcontracting, partnerships that require client-handoff, or scaling past the cap should be pushed back against.

## The goal

The owner's stated goal is a sustainable lifestyle practice that replaces or approximates current salary (~$6k/month target) within 5 years, while preserving time for family — specifically a young daughter. This is not a growth business. Optimize for:

- Long client retention (3–5 years per client)
- Few, high-quality relationships (2–4 active retainers)
- Bounded hours per client (8–15 hrs/month sustaining)
- Work that can be done during weekends and evenings while keeping the day job (for now)

Reject suggestions that prioritize growth, scale, or revenue maximization over these goals. Examples of bad advice to reject:

- "Hire junior consultants to scale"
- "Productize and sell SaaS"
- "Take on 10+ clients in year one"
- "Go full-time immediately"
- "Launch a certification program / training course"

## The target client — right fit

**Company profile:**

- LatAm-based SaaS company (primarily Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, Argentina, Chile)
- Running on AWS (or migrating to it)
- 15–150 employees
- Series A through early Series C funding
- Has existing or ambitioned US enterprise revenue
- Has at least one specific pain point: stuck deal in security review, signed contract requiring SOC 2, or upcoming enterprise sales cycle

**Buyer profile:**

- Primary: CTO or VP Engineering
- Secondary: Founder/CEO at smaller companies, CISO at larger ones
- Buyer understands that security is a real investment, not a sticker they can buy
- Buyer has budget and willingness to commit to 12-month engagement
- Buyer is willing to dedicate engineering time (15–25 hrs/week for 3 months during foundation)

## The target client — wrong fit (refuse these)

Actively refuse these clients, even if they offer money:

- Need a SOC 2 report in 30 days for a specific deal (physics makes this impossible)
- Want compliance theater — a badge without changing how they operate
- Need a hands-on security operations person running a SOC (this is advisory, not ops)
- Company has zero engineering bandwidth for remediation
- Enterprise (500+ employees) that should be hiring full-time CISO
- Deep HIPAA, PCI-DSS, or FedRAMP requirements — these require specialists
- Clients whose business relies on questionable data practices (credit scoring with no consent, scraping, adtech with dark patterns)

## The services

Exactly three offerings. No more.

### 1. Gap Assessment — the entry door

- **Fee:** $6,000 fixed
- **Duration:** 2–3 weeks
- **Deliverable:** 42-control scorecard, priority-ranked remediation roadmap, 90-minute findings review
- **Credit:** Fee credits toward retainer if client continues within 60 days
- **Purpose:** This is the trial. Most retainers start as gap assessments. Never skip this step with a new client.

### 2. vCISO Retainer — the main product

- **Fee:** $3,000–$5,000/month USD (final number set after gap assessment based on company size and complexity)
- **Term:** 12-month minimum
- **Scope — included:**
  - Monthly 90-min strategy session
  - Ownership of policy set (12–15 documents)
  - Up to 2 vendor questionnaire responses per month
  - Quarterly access reviews
  - Up to 1 customer security call per month
  - IR on-call during business hours
  - Audit coordination
  - Slack/email with 24-hour business-day SLA
- **Scope — NOT included (clarify up front, always):**
  - Audit fees (auditor is separate CPA firm, $20k–$40k/year, paid directly by client)
  - Pentest fees (separate firm, $8k–$20k/year, paid directly by client)
  - GRC platform subscriptions (Vanta, Drata, Secureframe — client pays vendor directly)
  - 24/7 on-call — this is a solo practice, not a SOC
  - Implementation engineering — the client's dev team implements; Veridata advises

### 3. One-off Projects — for clients with existing security leadership

- **Fee:** $8,000+ per project, scoped individually
- **Types:** AWS security review, pentest coordination, specific questionnaire response, IR tabletop facilitation
- **Purpose:** Fills revenue gaps in early months. Also a second entry path to retainer. In year 2+, deprioritize these if they don't convert to retainers.

## Pricing principles

- Published pricing. Opaque pricing is a red flag in this industry; the owner's positioning is anti-that.
- Never price the retainer below $2,500/month. Lower prices attract cash-strapped clients who drain time and churn in 6 months.
- Never discount more than 15% — if you discount more, the client saw the price as wrong, and you're about to have pricing problems forever.
- Founding-client discounts are allowed for the first 2 clients only, explicitly time-boxed ("first 3 months at $X").
- Invoice in USD. LatAm clients with US revenue expect this and it avoids FX drama.

## Things Veridata Pro does NOT do — ever

Hard nos. These come up in client conversations and should be refused consistently:

- **"Can you just write the answers we need to pass?"** — No. That's fraud. It also fails at the next customer review.
- **"Can you sign off that we're SOC 2 compliant?"** — No. Veridata is not a CPA firm and cannot issue SOC 2 attestations.
- **"Can you be our pentester?"** — No. Pentests must be independent third parties.
- **"Can you do this on contingency — only pay if we close the deal?"** — No. Too much client-side execution dependency.
- **"Can you guarantee we pass the audit?"** — No. 80% of audit failure is client-caused. The only guarantee offered is: "If our work product misses a control that causes an audit finding, we remediate at no charge."
- **"Can we use your logo on our website as a security advisor?"** — Not until a live retainer is 6+ months old, and only with case-by-case approval.
- **"Can you promise a 48-hour audit pass?"** — Absolutely not. This is physically impossible for Type II and a red flag in the market.

## Sales and conversation principles

When drafting emails, proposals, or advising on client calls:

**Lead with honesty, not enthusiasm.** Stressed buyers trust the advisor who tells them what's real, not the one promising the moon. "Most clients close their first stuck US deal within 30–60 days of starting work" is more persuasive than "we'll save your deal fast."

**Name the wrong fits early.** Telling a prospect "we're probably not the right fit for you" when true is a closing technique. It creates trust with the right-fit prospects listening.

**Never promise outcomes the client controls.** Deals, audit passes, questionnaire scores — these depend on the client's execution. Veridata guarantees the quality of its advice, not the outcome of the client's business.

**Default to narrower scope.** If a client asks for 10 things, offer to do 6 exceptionally. Scope creep is the primary failure mode of solo advisory practices.

**Refer out generously.** When a prospect needs something Veridata doesn't do (HIPAA specialist, full-time CISO, SOC operator), refer to a known contact. Referrals build network and come back.

## Voice in written communications

Applies to emails, proposals, blog posts, LinkedIn, and any client-facing prose.

- First-person singular ("I") — this is a solo practice, not a "we"
- Direct but not terse — warmth without filler
- Specific numbers over generalities ("6–8 retainer clients" vs "a select group")
- Acknowledge tradeoffs openly — don't pretend the work is easy
- No humble-brag about credentials — state them once (Deloitte, Accenture, Alithya, pharma compliance experience) and move on
- No jargon unless the reader is technical and the jargon is precise (vCISO, SOC 2 Type II, CloudTrail are fine; "synergies" and "transformational" are not)
- Portuguese and Spanish communications allowed and encouraged for LatAm clients; English for everything else including US buyers
