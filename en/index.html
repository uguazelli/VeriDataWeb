The Problem

When your team becomes the integration layer.

L01

Systems do not talk to each other.

CRM, ERP, ecommerce, spreadsheets, and internal tools all work separately. People bridge the gaps manually.

L02

Core systems are surrounded by manual work.

Whether it's an ERP, CRM, or Clinical EMR, your core software should run the operation — but teams still depend on exports, copy-paste, and spreadsheets.

L03

Automations break silently.

A workflow stops, a webhook changes, or a connector fails. The first alert is usually a customer, a missed report, or a finance problem.

Why It Matters

Most integrations look fine in the demo. The difference shows in production.

Validation fails. Data drifts. Retries are missing. Nobody gets alerted. Nobody owns the fix. When a workflow touches revenue, finance, inventory, or customer handoff, the integration layer needs more than a connector.

Increasingly, the integration layer also means cleaning up half-working AI-generated automations that pass the demo and fail in production.

What has to exist after go-live

Business outcome What it means in practice
Bad data stops at the door Required fields, source rules, and formats are checked before bad data moves downstream.
Recoverable failures recover Known temporary failures are retried automatically, with limits and visibility.
Every run leaves a trail Each workflow run logs source, payload, status, and failure reason — traceable end to end.
You hear about it first The owner is alerted before the customer, finance team, or ops team finds the issue.
Someone owns the workflow There is a named owner, a handoff rule, and a clear escalation path.
